Things to know before using some of your picks

MLB Stats API and Statcast (#12)
MLB's terms allow personal, non-commercial use only.
  • Fine: stating facts like "a 314-foot porch," with credit.
  • Not fine: pulling their data in bulk for monetized videos.
  • Fallback for history: Retrosheet is safer, but it requires its credit line.
Ticket and transit sign-ups (#11)
  • MTA Bus Time and NJ Transit: simple free keys.
  • Ticketmaster and SeatGeek: free, but ticket sellers limit how their data can be shown, so read their terms first.
Traffic cameras (#9)
Works, and a new find below makes it easier. Check NYC DOT's terms before you show camera images on screen.
StatsBomb (#13)
Free, but they require their credit on screen.
"Every building on this block in 1940"
It's on both of your lists. The 1940s tax photos exist, but I still haven't confirmed their license.
Old-map overlays and pedestrian counts
NYPL Map Warper was down the last time I checked. Pedestrian counts still have no confirmed counter near MSG.

The 125 links: what works

A beginner tip about sources: many links are brand-new GitHub copies of government data, made to promote a small website. You can spot them because they were created in 2026 and have 0 to 15 stars. They aren't fake, but for accurate stories, always credit and check the original source, like HUD for rents or NTSB for plane crashes, not the copy. These are marked Copy below.

1. Places, buildings and maps (most useful for us)

#44awesome-citygml (a list of free 3D city models)
It led me to the NYC 3D Building Model: every building in the city from a 2014 aerial survey, about 100 famous ones with detailed roofs. It's a 916 MB download.
Best findIt could replace our boxy neighbor buildings with real shapes. The catch: anything built after 2014 is missing.
#62NYU shadow maps
Precomputed building shadows for NYC, Chicago, LA, DC and Boston.
UsefulGreat for a "how much sun hits the Garden's plaza" video, or alongside Manhattanhenge. No license is listed, so we'd ask first.

2. Sports

#1SportsDataverse
Free tools for NBA, WNBA, college football and hockey data. One of them, sportyR, draws regulation fields, rinks and courts to exact size.
Best for blueprintsLay the rink over the baseball diamond for "MSG vs. Yankee Stadium to scale."
#2TheSportsDB
Teams, venues, logos and schedules from around the world.
Use with careWorks with the free test key. Crowd-sourced, so double-check facts. Logos are trademarks, so don't show them.
#114Equity in Athletics (US Dept. of Education)
How much money each college spends on and earns from sports.
FutureGreat for a "college stadiums" series.
#116Sports skills list
SkipCopy, weak.

6. Society and economy (future channels, not this one)

Solid official sources: UN Data (#74), UNESCO (#71), the UN Human Development Report (#22), the World Inequality Database (#109), Harvard's dataverse (#14, which I believe is its trade "Atlas," but the page needs a browser to confirm), WIOD trade tables (#29), OpenCorporates (#18), Pew (#106, needs a free account) and NYC trash tonnage (#70).

Fun angle from the trash data: "how many tons of trash Midtown makes."

Have limits: ACLED conflict data (#101) needs registration. OpenSanctions (#105) and Is It Safe To Travel (#102) are non-commercial only.

### Notes for the AI voice
- Try CapCut's built-in text-to-speech first (free, inside the app you already use). If it sounds flat, test a paid voice later and compare.
- Write numbers out in words in the voice text (as above), or the voice may read "42" oddly.
- Pause marks: put a line break after "Madison Square Garden." and after "this." for the beat before the reveal.

## Photos and newspapers: what you can use

- **Library of Congress photos** in `real photo references/penn station 1910` (1905 to 1942): most are public domain. Credits are in that folder's credits.md.
- **Demolition photos (1963 to 1966)**: most famous ones belong to newspapers and are not free to use. The Library of Congress HABS survey of Penn Station (photos taken before demolition) is public domain and a safe choice.
- **New York Times (your account)**: TimesMachine pages from 1929 and earlier are public domain in the US, so the **1910 opening-day coverage** is safe. **1963 pages are still under copyright**, and the Times subscription terms don't cover reposting. If you use one, show only a headline for a second or two while the voice comments on it. That's a fair use argument, not a guarantee. The free Library of Congress "Chronicling America" site has other public domain papers.
- **Google Earth Studio**: free for videos as long as the "Google Earth" credit (and the data credits it shows) stays on screen. Sign in at earth.google.com/studio with your Google account.
